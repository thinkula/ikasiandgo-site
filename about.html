Why this exists

Ikasi & Go was built by one person, for people trying to reach a language their family left behind.

Ugalde
ur · water alde · beside

The name means by the river. Like most Basque surnames, it was a house first, and the house was named for the land it stood on.

That is the thing about Basque names: they are little pieces of landscape carried across an ocean. Mendieta is a place of mountains. Zubizarreta is the old bridge. Etxeberria is simply the new house. Families in the Basque Country took the name of the house, and when they emigrated, the house came with them as a word.

A lot of us grew up with one of those words on our mail and no way to read the rest of the language it came from. Euskara has been spoken in the Pyrenees for thousands of years and is unrelated to any other language on earth. It survived Rome, empires, and a dictatorship that banned it. But for an English speaker, especially one an ocean away, finding a way in has never been simple.

This app is the tool I wanted: Basque explained in English, with the culture attached to every word.

How the words were made

The vocabulary is the whole product, so it got the attention. Every one of the 939 words and phrases was written by hand, with a pronunciation guide for English speakers, an example sentence, and a note explaining where the word comes from or how people actually use it.

Then it was checked, and checked again. Every headword and every example sentence is validated against Xuxen, the Basque spellchecker built on the standards of Euskaltzaindia, the Basque Language Academy. Glosses, grammar, and cultural claims were reviewed line by line, and corrections have already been shipped for hundreds of entries. When a note claims something about Basque history or etymology, it is meant to hold up.

It will not be perfect. Euskara has real regional variation and a living standard. If you find something wrong, tell me and it gets fixed for everyone, usually within days.

Who it is for

Anyone learning Euskara from English: the grandchild of emigrants, the person who married into a Basque family, the traveler headed to Donostia, the linguist who cannot resist a language isolate. You do not need Basque ancestry to be welcome here. You just need to be curious about one of the most remarkable languages in the world.
